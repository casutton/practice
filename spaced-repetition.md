# Comfort Levels and your Repertoire

**The short version:** every tune has a Comfort Level - how well you know it. Rating a tune after you practice it nudges that level up or down. Once a tune reaches "I know this one," it counts as part of your Repertoire.

There are no due dates. Nothing goes overdue, nothing nags you, and no tune is ever late. What you practice is your decision, made when you plan your week; the Comfort Levels are there to help you make it.

<div class="tip">
    <strong>Prefer to watch?</strong> <a href="/practice/how-to/what-you-can-play/">Find what tunes you know</a> covers this whole page in about a minute and a half.
</div>

## The three levels

<ul>
    <li><strong>Still learning</strong> - you're working it up; it isn't reliable yet.</li>
    <li><strong>I know this one</strong> - you can play it. This is the threshold for your Repertoire.</li>
    <li><strong>I know this cold</strong> - you could play it half asleep at the end of a long night.</li>
</ul>

There's also **Don't practice**, for tunes you want in the library but out of the way.

**Your Repertoire is every tune at "I know this one" or above.** That's the list you want when someone at a jam asks what you can play and your mind goes blank.

## How ratings move the level

When you finish a tune in a practice session you rate it. The rating is the only thing that moves a Comfort Level automatically.

<div class="flow-step">
    <span class="step-number">1</span>
    <strong>Needs Work</strong>
    <p>Drops the tune one Comfort Level. Not a reset to zero - one step back. A bad night shouldn't undo a year.</p>
</div>

<div class="flow-step">
    <span class="step-number">2</span>
    <strong>Almost</strong>
    <p>Holds the tune where it is, and breaks any run of "Got It"s you had going.</p>
</div>

<div class="flow-step">
    <span class="step-number">3</span>
    <strong>Got It</strong>
    <p>Three in a row move the tune up one Comfort Level. One good night isn't proof - you have to show it three times.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip">
    <strong>Be honest.</strong> The levels are only as useful as the ratings behind them. Marking something "Got It" because you'd like it to be true just gives you a Repertoire list you can't trust in the one moment you need it.
</div>

## Setting a level yourself

You don't have to wait for the ratings to get there. Open a tune, tap **Edit**, scroll to **Practice History**, and set the Comfort Level directly.

This is the quickest way to load in a repertoire you already have: add the tunes, mark the ones you know, and your Repertoire list is populated without practising all of them first.

## Seeing your Repertoire

Two routes, depending on where you are:

- **In the library** - open the filter menu and choose **Repertoire**. The list narrows to the tunes you know.
- **During a practice session** - the "Go straight to" card on the practice screen has a **Repertoire** door, so you can look without dropping out of your session.

## Haven't played in a while

A tune can be solidly in your Repertoire and still quietly slip away from you. **Haven't played in a while** collects the Repertoire tunes you haven't touched lately, and it's one of the pools you draw from when you plan a week.

Playing a tune resets its clock whether or not you rate it - so a tune you logged at a jam counts, even though it never got a rating.

To change the window, go to **Settings → Practice Settings → Configure Bucket Intervals**, and set **Unplayed for** under "Haven't Played In A While." The default is 30 days.

<div class="info">
    <strong>On the same screen</strong> you'll find per-level interval settings, left over from an earlier version of the app that scheduled tunes by due date. They no longer affect anything you'll see - the app doesn't schedule reviews any more. Only the "Unplayed for" setting is live.
</div>

## Turning it off

If you'd rather not think about any of this, go to **Settings → Practice Settings** and switch **Spaced Repetition** off. The Comfort Level controls disappear from the tune page, the related library filters go away, and so does the interval screen. Your tunes, sessions and notes are all untouched - you just stop being asked how well you know things.

Switch it back on and everything returns as it was.
